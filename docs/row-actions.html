Actions on the row

Option B as chosen — eye at the end, hatch behind hidden rows — with the question of what else belongs within reach. Zoom to the layer is the obvious candidate; the rest is a judgement about how many icons a row can carry before it stops being a list.

The constraint that decides this: nothing may appear or disappear on hover in a way that changes the row's size. That is what made the sidebar jump under the pointer — the thumbnail you were about to click moved. Every option below reserves its space whether the buttons are visible or not.

A · Zoom and eye, always

Three buttons, permanent: fly to the layer, draw or hide it, and everything else in the menu. The narrowest honest answer — one more icon than you have now.

Recommendation: D. It answers your question — zoom is on every row, permanently, next to the eye — without spending a third of the panel on icons that mean nothing until you hover them to read a tooltip.

The part worth arguing for is the strip under the selected row. A table of contents has exactly one row you are working on, and that is the row that can afford twenty more pixels. It also solves something icons cannot: Browse 23 images says what it will do and how much of it there is, which no glyph does. And the actions can differ by layer — imagery gets Browse, a vector layer gets Style and Table, a feed that is off gets Connect — instead of every row carrying every button so that one kind of layer can use it.
